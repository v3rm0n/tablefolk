How Sasku works

Four browsers.
One private table.

Players connect to each other, build one encrypted deck, and verify the same game. Each browser can read its own hand, while everyone can check what happens at the table.

01 Connect the players ↘ 02 Play the game ↘

01 / Connection

First, the browsers find each other.

A relay introduces the players. Once the links are ready, the four browsers exchange game messages directly.

    02 / Introduction

    Exchange connection details

    Nostr relays carry encrypted offers and network candidates, so browsers can find a WebRTC route. The relays do not carry game moves.

  3. Six browser-to-browser links
    03 / Peer network

    Verify and connect

    Every pair forms a WebRTC link and checks signed identities. The four players agree on the roster and rules, then synchronize their signed history.

✓

Now the table is connected. Game actions travel between the browsers over authenticated peer links. The relay’s job was the introduction.

Some networks block browser-to-browser links. This build has no TURN fallback, so a table cannot start if those links fail.

02 / The game

Then they run the round together.

There is no separate dealer. Each browser keeps its own secrets and checks every public step.

    03 / Play

    Reveal one card at a time

    Bids and trump are public. When you play, you sign the move and reveal that card’s missing share. Everyone opens the played card and updates the same trick; the rest of your hand stays hidden.

    04 / Audit

    Check the finished hand

    After the ninth trick, all cards are disclosed. Every browser checks ownership, bids, following suit, and scoring against the signed record. Hidden-hand violations can be caught at this final audit.

The privacy boundary

Your unplayed cards stay in your browser.

The public record includes the roster, bids, trump, proofs, played cards, and scores. Those clues can narrow what another player might hold, but they do not reveal the unplayed cards themselves.

♠Only you can open

Your unplayed hand and your private key share.

✓Everyone can verify

The public actions, opened cards, and shuffle proofs.

Current build

One experimental round.

All four players must stay connected. On reload, a browser rechecks saved signatures and shuffle proofs before rebuilding its hand from its local key. The one-tab demo uses local message delivery in place of WebRTC; the game steps are the same.

The shuffle proof backend still awaits independent review. This privacy design depends on its cryptographic assumptions and on players keeping their own browsers and keys private.
